## Hardware Extensions for KB Project: OAN Subsystem

- [`oan-hardware-appliance.md`](oan-hardware-appliance.md) — **Proposed, not implemented (v0.2).** Out-of-Band Attestation Node: a physically independent appliance (SBC + STM32 safety controller + ESP32 comms processor + TPM root of trust + relay fencing) that supervises and attests Kernel Borderlands from outside the host's trust boundary, addressing the one failure mode software-only same-host watchdogging can't structurally cover: total kernel compromise. Continues `kb-checker`'s external-watchdog role but is scoped as its own future subsystem, not a `kb-checker` extension. Includes an updated bench-buildable bill of materials, a production-appliance concept, and cluster-support direction (future work, detailed in `oan-fms.md`); open questions (repo placement, wire protocol, failure semantics, internal SBC/STM32/ESP32 protocol) explicitly unresolved. Supersedes the earlier `kbc-hardware-watchdog.md`.
- [`oan-fms.md`](oan-fms.md) — **Proposed, not implemented.** Fabric Management Service: the fleet-orchestration sub-service of OAN, expanding its "Cluster Support" section into a Fabric → Colony → Family → Node hierarchy with discovery, policy inheritance, health monitoring, event aggregation, cross-host correlation, and recovery coordination. Explicitly scoped to come *after* a working single-host OAN prototype, not before. Resolved (see `oan-hardware-appliance.md` §3.1): FMS is classified non-security-critical under a Local Autonomy Principle (every node protects itself with zero dependency on FMS) and communicates only over a separate Fabric Management Plane (dedicated VLAN/NIC, signed summaries only) that structurally cannot reach the OOB Trust Plane used for attestation/recovery — closing what were previously two open risks. Cross-host correlation ownership and the fleet wire protocol remain open.
- [`oan-cheap.md`](oan-cheap.md) — **Proposed, not implemented (alt.).** Cost-reduced BOM appendix to `oan-hardware-appliance.md`'s Model B shared-chassis build — swaps in cheaper parts (Pi Zero 2 W, bare ESP32/STM32 modules, shared relay board, dropped display) that don't touch OAN's threat model, tags each as "free" or "trade-off." Brings a Pentagon (5-host) chassis from $234–$383 down to $114–$168, ~$11–$17 per person for a 10-person team. Explicitly rejects the one trade-off that looked biggest on paper — discrete TPM → firmware TPM (§3.7): not practically free on a Pi-based chassis (needs an unproven OP-TEE/TPM2 port), only genuinely free on an Intel N100 SBC, and even there weakens the design's hardware-rooted trust guarantee for a saving of just $4–$8 per host. Does not change architecture, threat model, or `oan-hardware-appliance.md`'s open questions — pricing only.
